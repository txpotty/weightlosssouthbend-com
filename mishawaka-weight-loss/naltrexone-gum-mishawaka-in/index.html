    How Naltrexone Gum Works for Patients in Mishawaka, IN

    How Naltrexone Gum Works in Mishawaka

    Naltrexone blocks opioid receptors, and the 50 mg tablet has been FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder for decades. In gum form, a compounding pharmacy blends a measured dose into a chewable base. Some of the drug may be absorbed through the lining of the mouth while you chew, and the rest is swallowed with saliva. That is the idea. Head-to-head data against tablets does not exist yet.

    Who Naltrexone Gum Is For

    Adults who would be reasonable candidates for naltrexone but dislike swallowing pills are the main group. Some ask about it as a low dose wellness option. Others want help with cravings as part of a broader plan, and like the idea of chewing a piece before a situation where cravings usually hit, such as a dinner out or the drive home from work. Opioid users are not candidates. Significant liver disease usually points to a different plan.

    Dosing and Use in Mishawaka

    Your provider sets the strength of each piece and how often to use it. A typical instruction is to chew slowly for roughly 20 to 30 minutes and then discard the piece rather than swallowing it. One piece a day is common. Other plans use it as needed, about an hour before a situation where cravings tend to appear, with a daily maximum written on your label. Compounded strengths vary, so follow the label exactly.

    What to Expect in the First Weeks

    The first week is mostly about learning the routine. Some people get mild nausea or a headache when they start any form of naltrexone, and a few notice lighter sleep for several nights. These usually ease within two weeks. The taste takes some getting used to. At a check-in around week two, your provider asks how many pieces you are using, whether the timing works and whether your gums feel sore.

    Side Effects of Naltrexone Gum in Mishawaka

    The side effects match other forms of naltrexone and include nausea, headache, dizziness and trouble sleeping. Chewing brings a couple of extras, such as a bitter aftertaste and sore gums. Opioids are the serious concern. Anyone who takes opioids, even now and then, can go into withdrawal on naltrexone, and opioid pain pills may stop working. Report yellowing skin, dark urine or severe stomach pain right away.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your questionnaire asks about current medications, opioid use, liver health, pregnancy and breastfeeding, plus any major medical conditions. Two drugs get named directly, tramadol and codeine syrup. A recent liver panel showing ALT and AST may be requested when your history raises a flag. Jaw trouble matters too. Anyone with TMJ pain or fresh dental work may do better on capsules.

    How Naltrexone Gum Compares in Mishawaka

    Standard naltrexone tablets have decades of research and FDA approval for alcohol and opioid use disorder. LDN capsules sit at the other end, compounded and off-label at a few milligrams a night. The gum is a newer compounded format with limited published data, chosen mostly for convenience or because swallowing pills is a problem. Weight loss is a different story. GLP-1 medications like semaglutide and tirzepatide have much better weight loss data than naltrexone in any form.
