    How Low Dose Naltrexone Works for Patients in South Bend, IN

    How Low Dose Naltrexone Works in South Bend

    Standard naltrexone is a 50 mg tablet, FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder, and it blocks opioid receptors for most of the day. Low dose naltrexone drops that to somewhere between 1.5 and 4.5 mg, taken at night. The block lasts a few hours. Researchers think the short blockade may lead the body to turn up its own endorphin signaling once the drug wears off, though that idea is still being tested. LDN also seems to quiet microglia, immune cells in the brain and spinal cord linked to pain and inflammation. That is the theory, not settled science.

    Who Low Dose Naltrexone Is For

    Most people who ask about LDN live with fibromyalgia or a long-running inflammatory condition that has not responded well to the usual treatments. The most quoted evidence comes from small trials in fibromyalgia and Crohn's disease. Some patients also ask about it alongside a weight management plan, though it is not a weight loss drug. Anyone taking opioids is out. Your provider will ask what you hope to change, such as pain scores or the number of flare days, and whether that goal matches what the studies actually measured.

    LDN Dosing and Schedule in South Bend

    A common start is 1.5 mg at bedtime, in a compounded capsule or a liquid you measure with an oral syringe. Every one to two weeks the dose usually steps up by about 1.5 mg until it reaches 3 to 4.5 mg. Night dosing lines the short receptor block up with sleep. Vivid dreams can follow. If they keep you up, your provider may move the dose to the morning, which a fair number of patients end up preferring after the first month.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Weeks one and two are mostly about sleep. Expect strange, very vivid dreams, along with a few nights of lighter sleep, especially right after the dose goes up. By weeks three and four most patients are near their target dose. Patients who respond usually describe a gradual change between week 4 and week 12, such as fewer bad pain days, rather than one morning when everything is different. Check-ins happen at each dose step. Around month three, you and the provider decide whether it has earned a place in your routine.

    Side Effects of LDN in South Bend

    Vivid dreams and insomnia top the list of side effects in published studies, with headache and mild nausea showing up a little less often. Most of it fades within two weeks of a dose change. The bigger issue is opioids. LDN can block opioid pain medicine from working and can push someone who takes opioids regularly into withdrawal, so even a single tramadol tablet needs to be mentioned. Report dark urine or yellowing skin through secure messaging the same day.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake form asks for every prescription and supplement you take, with a specific question about tramadol and codeine cough syrup because people forget those count as opioids. Liver history matters too. Active liver disease or a current pregnancy usually means LDN is not the right call. Nothing is prescribed unless the provider thinks the likely benefit beats the risk in your case. Some patients are asked to upload a recent liver panel showing ALT and AST values.

    How LDN Compares to Related Options in South Bend

    Full-dose naltrexone at 50 mg is FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder and blocks receptors around the clock, which is a different effect from the short nightly block of LDN. Naltrexone gum is another compounded format, newer and with even less published data. On weight loss, GLP-1 drugs win easily. In the STEP trials, semaglutide 2.4 mg averaged roughly 15 percent body weight loss over 68 weeks, and LDN has nothing close to that evidence. Your provider can check whether LDN can sit alongside a GLP-1 or another program you already use.
